# My Safety Plan

> **AI Instructions:** This is a youth-adapted version of the safety plan for users under 18.
> Use simple, direct language (5th–6th grade reading level). Avoid legal jargon. Replace
> "partner" with "the person hurting you" or "the person who is unsafe." Replace "advocate"
> with "a grown-up whose job is to help." Walk the youth through one section at a time.
> Validate their courage. Reassure them frequently that it is not their fault. If they
> indicate immediate danger, stop the plan and direct them to call 911, text 911, or call
> Childhelp (1-800-422-4453). If they express suicidal thoughts, route to 988 or Crisis
> Text Line (text HOME to 741741). Never pressure them to share more than they want to.

---

## Before You Start

**This plan is yours.** It is private and it belongs to you. You get to decide what goes in
it. You can skip any part that does not feel right. You can come back to it later.

**Keep this plan somewhere safe** — not on a phone, tablet, or computer that the person
hurting you can check. Some ideas:
- A friend's house
- In your locker at school
- In a book or folder where no one would look
- A private email account only you know about

**If you are in danger right now:**
- Call **911** (you can also text 911 in many places)
- Call **Childhelp: 1-800-422-4453** (free, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week)
- Text **HOME to 741741** (Crisis Text Line — free and private)

## Part 7 — If Someone Is Hurting Me in a Relationship

If a boyfriend, girlfriend, or partner is hurting you, controlling you, or making you
feel afraid — that is not okay, and it is not love.

**Signs that a relationship is NOT healthy:**
- They tell you what to wear or who to talk to
- They check your phone or social media without asking
- They get angry or jealous when you spend time with friends
- They threaten to hurt you, themselves, or share private pictures of you
- They push, hit, grab, or hurt you physically
- They pressure you to do sexual things you do not want to do
- They make you feel like everything is your fault

**What healthy relationships look like:**
- You feel safe
- You can say no and they respect it
- You can hang out with your own friends
- They do not try to control you
- Disagreements happen, but no one gets hurt or threatened

**If you are in an unhealthy or abusive relationship:**
- Call **loveisrespect: 1-866-331-9474** or text LOVEIS to 22522
- This is free, private, and just for teens

## For Grown-Ups Helping a Young Person with This Plan

> **AI Instructions:** When an adult (parent, counselor, teacher, advocate) is helping a
> young person build this plan, use this section to guide them. The young person should
> lead — the adult's job is to support, not to direct or pressure.

If you are a counselor, teacher, advocate, or trusted adult helping a young person
fill out this plan:

**Your role:**
- **Let them lead.** Ask open-ended questions, but let them decide what goes in the plan.
- **Go at their pace.** Do not rush or push. Some sections may be too hard to fill out
  today — that is okay.
- **Believe them.** If they disclose abuse, respond with *"I believe you. This is not
  your fault."*
- **Be honest about reporting.** If you are a mandatory reporter, explain what that
  means before they share: *"I want you to know that if you tell me someone is hurting
  you, my job says I need to tell someone who can help keep you safe. I will not do
  anything without telling you first."*
- **Check in on emotions.** Safety planning can bring up hard feelings. Watch for signs
  of distress and take breaks when needed.
- **Store it safely.** Help them find a safe place for this plan where the person causing
  harm cannot find it.

**If the young person discloses abuse during safety planning:**
1. Stay calm. Thank them for trusting you.
2. Say: *"I believe you. This is not your fault. I am going to help."*
3. If you are a mandatory reporter, follow the reporting procedures in
   `templates/mandatory-reporter-checklist.md`
4. If you are not a mandatory reporter, you can still call the Childhelp hotline
   (1-800-422-4453) for guidance on how to help
