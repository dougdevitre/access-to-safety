# Mandatory Reporter Checklist

> **AI Instructions:** When a user identifies as a mandatory reporter (or describes a scenario
> that triggers mandatory reporting obligations), guide them through the appropriate reporting
> type below. Ask clarifying questions to determine which type of abuse is suspected, then
> walk them through that section's checklist. Always lead with the reporting hotline number.
> Reassure reporters that they are protected by law when reporting in good faith. Remind them
> they do NOT need proof — only reasonable suspicion. Do not encourage them to investigate.
> If multiple types of abuse are suspected (e.g., a child who is also being trafficked),
> walk through each relevant section. Adapt for the reporter's state if known; the Missouri
> defaults below are the reference implementation.

---

## Who Is a Mandatory Reporter?

Mandatory reporters are professionals who are **legally required** to report suspected abuse
or neglect. In Missouri (RSMo §210.115, §565.188, §630.162, §192.2405), mandatory reporters include:

- Teachers, school administrators, school counselors
- Healthcare providers (physicians, nurses, dentists, EMTs, therapists)
- Licensed counselors, social workers, psychologists
- Childcare workers and foster parents
- Law enforcement officers
- Clergy (in certain contexts)
- Residential care facility staff
- Day program or shelter workers
- Coroners and medical examiners

> **Not sure if you are a mandatory reporter?** Many states have expanded mandatory reporter
> lists. When in doubt, report anyway — **any person** may make a report in good faith, and
> all reporters are protected by law.

## Quick Decision Guide

**What type of abuse or neglect do you suspect?**

| If you suspect... | Go to |
|-------------------|-------|
| A child (under 18) is being abused or neglected | **Section 1: Child Abuse & Neglect** |
| An elderly person (60+) is being abused or neglected | **Section 2: Elder Abuse & Neglect** |
| A vulnerable adult (disability, mental illness, in residential care) is being abused | **Section 3: Vulnerable Adult Abuse** |
| Domestic violence where children are exposed | **Section 4: DV with Children Present** |
| Human trafficking (any age) | **Section 5: Human Trafficking** |
| Sexual abuse or assault disclosed in a professional setting | **Section 6: Sexual Abuse Disclosure** |
| More than one category applies | Start with the most immediate safety concern, then proceed through each relevant section |

## Section 1 — Child Abuse & Neglect

### Report To

```
Missouri Child Abuse/Neglect Hotline: 1-800-392-3738 (24/7)
Online reporting: https://dss.mo.gov/cd/keeping-kids-safe/
```

> **For other states:** Find your state's hotline at Childhelp (1-800-422-4453) or
> childwelfare.gov/organizations

### When to Report

You must report when you have **reasonable cause to suspect** that a child:
- Has been subjected to physical abuse, sexual abuse, or emotional abuse
- Is being neglected (inadequate food, shelter, clothing, medical care, supervision)
- Is in conditions that would reasonably result in abuse or neglect
- Has disclosed abuse or neglect to you

**You do NOT need:**
- [ ] Proof that abuse occurred
- [ ] The child to disclose directly
- [ ] Permission from a supervisor to report (you are individually liable)
- [ ] To identify the abuser
- [ ] To investigate further — that is the Division's job

### Pre-Report Checklist — What to Have Ready

Gather what you **already know** before calling. Do not delay the report to collect missing information.

- [ ] Child's name (or identifying description)
- [ ] Child's approximate age or date of birth
- [ ] Child's address or school
- [ ] Parent/guardian names (if known)
- [ ] Parent/guardian contact information (if known)
- [ ] **What you observed or were told** — be specific:
  - Physical indicators (bruises, burns, untreated injuries)
  - Behavioral indicators (fearfulness, withdrawal, aggression, age-inappropriate sexual knowledge)
  - Child's exact words (write them down verbatim)
  - Environmental conditions (unsanitary home, lack of food, no supervision)
- [ ] Dates and times of your observations
- [ ] Your name and contact information (kept confidential — not shared with the family)
- [ ] Names of other professionals who may have relevant information

### After Reporting

- [ ] Children's Division screens the report within 24 hours
- [ ] If accepted: investigation begins within 24–72 hours (non-emergency) or immediately (emergency)
- [ ] You may be contacted for follow-up
- [ ] You will NOT be told the outcome (this is normal — confidentiality protects the family)
- [ ] Document that you made the report: date, time, name of intake worker, any reference number

### What NOT to Do

- [ ] Do NOT investigate on your own
- [ ] Do NOT confront the suspected abuser
- [ ] Do NOT interview the child beyond what they volunteer
- [ ] Do NOT promise the child secrecy — say: *"I need to tell someone whose job it is to help keep you safe"*
- [ ] Do NOT delay — report as soon as reasonably possible
- [ ] Do NOT wait for a supervisor's approval (your obligation is personal and non-delegable)

### Legal Protection

> Missouri law provides **immunity from civil and criminal liability** for reporters acting
> in good faith (RSMo §210.135). You are protected even if the investigation does not
> confirm abuse.

## Section 2 — Elder Abuse & Neglect

### Report To

```
Missouri Elder Abuse & Neglect Hotline: 1-800-392-0210 (24/7)
Missouri Adult Abuse & Neglect Hotline: 1-800-392-0210
```

> **National:** Eldercare Locator: 1-800-677-1116
> **For other states:** Find your state's Adult Protective Services at napsa-now.org

### Types of Elder Abuse to Watch For

| Type | Indicators |
|------|-----------|
| **Physical abuse** | Unexplained bruises, fractures, burns; fear of caregiver; conflicting explanations for injuries |
| **Emotional/psychological abuse** | Withdrawal, depression, fearfulness, agitation; caregiver belittling or threatening |
| **Neglect** | Malnutrition, dehydration, unsanitary conditions, untreated medical needs, pressure sores |
| **Self-neglect** | Inability to care for self, hoarding, refusing medical care, unsafe living conditions |
| **Financial exploitation** | Unusual bank withdrawals, missing belongings, sudden changes to will or power of attorney, unpaid bills despite resources |
| **Sexual abuse** | Unexplained STIs, bruising in genital area, behavioral changes, fear of specific person |
| **Abandonment** | Desertion at hospital, nursing facility, or public location |

### Pre-Report Checklist

- [ ] Older adult's name and approximate age
- [ ] Location (home address, facility name)
- [ ] Relationship of suspected abuser to the older adult (if known)
- [ ] What you observed or were told — specific indicators
- [ ] Whether the older adult is in immediate danger
- [ ] Whether the older adult has cognitive impairment (dementia, confusion)
- [ ] Names of other involved parties (caregivers, family, facility staff)
- [ ] Your name and contact information
- [ ] Dates and times of observations

### After Reporting

- [ ] Adult Protective Services (APS) will investigate
- [ ] If the person is in a facility, the state licensing agency may also investigate
- [ ] APS may coordinate with law enforcement for criminal matters
- [ ] Document that you made the report: date, time, intake worker, reference number

### Special Considerations

- An older adult may refuse help (they have the right to self-determination unless legally incapacitated)
- If you suspect financial exploitation, APS may coordinate with the prosecutor's office
- If the older adult is in a licensed care facility, also report to the state Department of Health and Senior Services

## Section 3 — Vulnerable Adult Abuse

### Report To

```
Missouri Adult Abuse & Neglect Hotline: 1-800-392-0210 (24/7)
For abuse in licensed facilities: Missouri DHSS complaint line: 573-751-6303
```

### Who Is a Vulnerable Adult?

A person 18 or older who:
- Has a physical or developmental disability
- Has a mental illness or cognitive impairment
- Is in a residential care facility, group home, or day program
- Is otherwise unable to protect their own interests

### Pre-Report Checklist

- [ ] Individual's name and approximate age
- [ ] Location (home, facility name and address)
- [ ] Type of vulnerability (disability, mental illness, residential care)
- [ ] What you observed or were told
- [ ] Whether the individual is in immediate danger
- [ ] Suspected abuser's identity and relationship (if known)
- [ ] Whether the individual can communicate and advocate for themselves
- [ ] Your name, contact information, and professional role
- [ ] Dates and times of observations

### Key Differences from Elder Abuse Reporting

- Vulnerable adults may have guardians — abuse by a guardian is still reportable
- Facilities have additional reporting requirements under licensing regulations
- If the person is in a state-operated or funded program, the Missouri Quality Improvement Organization (QIO) may also need to be notified

## Section 4 — Domestic Violence with Children Present

### Report To

When children are **witnessing or exposed to** domestic violence, this may constitute a
mandatory report for child abuse/neglect in many jurisdictions.

```
Missouri Child Abuse/Neglect Hotline: 1-800-392-3738 (24/7)
```

### When Exposure to DV Triggers Mandatory Reporting

Report when a child:
- Has witnessed physical violence between caregivers
- Has been physically harmed during a DV incident (even accidentally)
- Shows behavioral or emotional signs of trauma from exposure
- Discloses seeing or hearing violence at home
- Lives in a home where weapons are used or threatened in DV incidents

### What to Document Before Reporting

- [ ] Child's name, age, school
- [ ] What the child said or what you observed (exact words)
- [ ] Whether the child has visible injuries
- [ ] Whether the child expressed fear of going home
- [ ] Whether you know the DV situation (who is the victim, who is the aggressor)
- [ ] Whether the non-offending parent is aware you are reporting
- [ ] Whether services are already involved (DV advocate, CASA, prior reports)

### Important Nuances

- **Do NOT blame the DV victim for the children's exposure.** The abuser's violence is the cause.
- The non-offending parent (DV survivor) may be afraid that CPS involvement will result in losing their children — this fear is valid and should be acknowledged
- Coordinate with DV advocates when possible — they can support the survivor through the CPS process
- Some states have explicitly stated that DV exposure alone does NOT constitute neglect by the non-offending parent — know your jurisdiction

## Section 5 — Human Trafficking

### Report To

```
National Human Trafficking Hotline: 1-888-373-7888 (24/7)
Text: 233733 (BEFREE)
Online: humantraffickinghotline.org
Report to FBI: tips.fbi.gov
```

> **For minors being trafficked:** Also report to the Child Abuse/Neglect Hotline (1-800-392-3738)
> because trafficking of a minor is child abuse.

### Indicators of Trafficking

**Labor trafficking indicators:**
- Person cannot leave workplace freely
- Lives and works in the same place (especially in isolated conditions)
- Has documents confiscated by employer
- Excessive work hours with little or no pay
- Signs of physical abuse or malnourishment
- Coached or scripted responses

**Sex trafficking indicators:**
- Minor involved in commercial sex (always trafficking, regardless of apparent "consent")
- Signs of physical abuse, branding, or tattooing
- Restricted communication or movement
- Someone else controls their money, ID, or phone
- Cannot speak for themselves; another person speaks on their behalf
- Hotel keys, large amounts of cash, multiple phones

### Pre-Report Checklist

- [ ] Location where you encountered the individual
- [ ] Physical description of the individual (if name is unknown)
- [ ] Whether the individual is a minor
- [ ] What you observed that raised concerns
- [ ] Description of anyone who appeared to be controlling the individual
- [ ] License plate numbers, vehicle descriptions, business names (if applicable)
- [ ] Whether the individual appeared to be in immediate danger
- [ ] Whether you believe labor trafficking, sex trafficking, or both

### Safety Warning

- Do NOT attempt to rescue the person yourself — this can escalate danger
- Do NOT confront the suspected trafficker
- Do NOT take photographs if it would put anyone at risk
- If you believe someone is in immediate danger, call 911

## Section 6 — Sexual Abuse Disclosure in Professional Settings

### Report To

The reporting destination depends on the victim's age and vulnerability:

| Victim | Report to |
|--------|-----------|
| Child (under 18) | Child Abuse/Neglect Hotline: 1-800-392-3738 |
| Vulnerable adult | Adult Abuse Hotline: 1-800-392-0210 |
| Adult (not vulnerable) | Encourage but cannot mandate; provide RAINN: 1-800-656-4673 |

### When a Professional Receives a Disclosure

- [ ] Believe the person. Respond with: *"Thank you for telling me. I believe you."*
- [ ] Do NOT ask probing questions — let them share what they choose to share
- [ ] Document the exact words they used (do not paraphrase)
- [ ] Explain your reporting obligation clearly and compassionately:
  *"Because of my role as [your role], I am required by law to report what you've told me to [agency]. This is to make sure you are safe."*
- [ ] Provide a warm handoff to a specialized advocate (RAINN, local rape crisis center) if possible
- [ ] For minors: follow Section 1 immediately
- [ ] For adults who are not vulnerable: you cannot mandate a report, but you can:
  - Provide the RAINN hotline (1-800-656-4673)
  - Offer to stay with them while they call
  - Explain their options (medical exam, reporting to police, counseling)
  - Respect their autonomy

### What NOT to Do

- Do NOT ask "Are you sure?" or "Why didn't you tell someone sooner?"
- Do NOT minimize ("At least it wasn't worse")
- Do NOT promise to keep it secret if you are a mandatory reporter
- Do NOT share the disclosure with anyone other than the appropriate reporting agency and, if applicable, your supervisor for the purpose of making the report

## Multi-Type Situations

When more than one category applies (e.g., an elderly person being trafficked, a child
witnessing DV who also shows signs of direct abuse), report to **each** relevant hotline.
You do not need to determine which "type" of abuse is primary — the investigating agencies
will coordinate.

**Priority order when multiple reports are needed:**
1. Immediate physical danger → 911
2. Child abuse/neglect → 1-800-392-3738
3. Elder/vulnerable adult abuse → 1-800-392-0210
4. Human trafficking → 1-888-373-7888
